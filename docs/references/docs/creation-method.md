# Practice guide

A global prompt carries two layers across projects. **The technical layer** tells the model how to use current tools, save files, and verify results. Update the relevant instructions when these arrangements change. **The literary layer for personal RSI** expresses what matters to you and how you want to work with the model. Yinyang starts with this layer. Choose views you want to adopt and draw on your experience and language to write six accounts that give the collaboration direction across projects. Revisit them when practice changes your understanding.

You can use Yinyang for personal recursive self-improvement (RSI) starting with something you already do or something you want to understand. An essay, a conversation, an impression from daily life, or a tool you use can all provide a starting point. The philosophical essays are optional reading.

From the same material, you and a language model can develop two independent things. A **view** articulates a judgment or commitment and the reasons for it. A **working model** describes the person, the counterpart, their cooperation, and the conditions under which it works and changes. Develop each for its own use. Where a working account draws on a view, cite the view and explain with your own material how it applies in your situation.

## Start from available material and practice

An essay, story, piece of music, design, or program can express how someone understands the world, what they value, and how they live and create with others. A work gives these relations a form people can encounter, discuss, and change. A language model can help you interpret the work, its making, and its effects. Making and using the work can also change your earlier understanding.

Philosophy has a broad sense here. Reflection on understanding, values, and ways of acting runs through personal creation. A story can embody a judgment in its own form. Articulating that judgment and the reasons for it can develop a view you can use elsewhere. So “articulated philosophy” in the table includes views made explicit in this way, and developing philosophy can begin by bringing out what is implicit in an existing work or practice.

The practice connects **what you create, the model as your creative partner, and the environment in which you work together**. Philosophy helps articulate meanings, values, and reasons. Psychological understanding helps you interpret experiences and patterns in your own and other people's activity. Engineering helps make a chosen arrangement usable and its effects observable. All three can contribute to the work, cooperation, and environment. [Philosophy, psychology, and engineering](local-source-and-website.md#philosophy-psychology-and-engineering-develop-the-missing-connection) develops these relations.

| Material or practice already available | Connection still needed and useful next contribution |
| --- | --- |
| Articulated philosophy or an adopted direction, with little personal grounding and no usable model environment | Connect the philosophy with event history and everyday life. Prepare the environment needed to develop and use that understanding with a model. |
| Relevant psychological understanding, conversations, or life materials, with neither articulated philosophy nor a usable model environment | Develop a grounded account and clarify what judgments or commitments the material supports. Make the necessary model assistance available. |
| An environment and experience using models, with personal understanding and philosophy still implicit | Interpret experience, preferences, and concepts together. Articulate the views they suggest and revise the arrangement through use. |
| Articulated philosophy and grounded psychological understanding, with no usable implementation | Decide which relations matter in an actual activity and realize them in the tools or arrangements you can use. |
| Articulated philosophy and a working environment, with little concrete personal grounding | Read conversations and life materials in light of the philosophy. Condense a grounded personal account and revise it through current use. |
| Grounded psychological understanding and a working environment, with philosophy still implicit | Articulate the understanding, values, and direction already implicit in the practice. |

The six combinations describe starting conditions. Writing that starts from literature, logic, aesthetics, or engineering philosophy can all develop this practice, in an order guided by the material already available. Prepare the files, retrieval, tools, or ways of checking results that the next contribution needs. A model can help frame a question, compare possible interpretations, carry out a change, and inspect its effects. You can begin useful work while your interpretation is still provisional.

## Form views and working models from your material

Write your own views, draw out a judgment implicit in your work or experience, or ask a language model to extract views from writing you read. You can select a few of Yinyang's [Core views](core-views.md) to adopt, combine them with other sources, and add your own judgments. Keep the source and the reasons for your adoption or change clear. [An example and a commons](example-and-commons.md) discusses adoption, combination, and independent authorship.

When developing a working model, ask what in the same material should change the way you and a model work together. Your materials may include a philosophical commitment, an impression of a conversation, a choice you made, or a result you observed. The model can reconstruct the relevant history, compare explanations, and draft a description. You contribute lived experience, purposes, objections, and judgment. A stated preference can guide an arrangement while its explanation remains open. An existing arrangement can reflect a constraint or default, so check what it tells you about the person's wishes.

For example, someone who always works at one desk may prefer it, need the equipment there, or rarely have had another option. Ask about a genuinely available alternative or try a reversible change that preserves the relevant equipment. If the evidence still supports several explanations, keep the account conditional. You can improve the workspace and leave the broader explanation open. [Forming and revising working models](../design-and-revision.md) develops this kind of understanding into six connected, usable accounts.

## Philosophy and the personal environment revise one another

Put a supported relation into practice. For computer-mediated work, describe the relevant activity, the conditions in which it occurs, how one step carries into the next, and the effects you can observe. You and the model can share the work of defining the problem, describing it, making the change, and checking the result. Files, interfaces, and tools make a chosen relation usable when their behavior serves the activity. The [environment writing guide](../formation/environment.md#derive-behavior-in-context) develops this connection.

Make the views and accounts relevant to everyday work available to the model, for example in its standing instructions. Have it use them to adjust a tool or an arrangement for something you already do. Notice a difficulty, propose an explanation in ordinary language, ask the model for a counterexample, and try it against what happens. Ask what became easier, what cost appeared, what you learned, and whether working together in this way supports what you want to develop. Use that evidence to revise the relevant understanding or arrangement. Methods that prove useful across cases can become tools or Skills.

Either philosophy or an existing environment can give the first direction. When usable results from both are available, they can also change the method that organized the work. If a difficulty keeps recurring, revise how you discover or address it. On the next occasion, see whether the changed method leads to an earlier discovery or a better response. This is the return that matters for strong RSI. It can also develop intuition, embodied reasoning, and reasoning in everyday language. The [RSI essay](literary-recursive-improvement.md#from-rewriting-words-to-changing-the-next-activity) explains the mechanism, and [its reading example](literary-recursive-improvement.md#how-one-reading-changes-the-way-reading-is-improved) follows an actual change in method.

### Direct model writing

Give your model the material relevant to the inquiry and any current accounts you want to revise. The [How to Write the Six Accounts](../formation/overview.md) help it organize and condense the material directly. Keep the six working accounts in your private `references/situated/`, and supporting material and source locators in `references/personal/`. The [package guide](skill-package.md#common-and-situated-content) explains the file layout. A private repository can preserve the versions you need. As models improve, you can ask them to reconsider the original material and its synthesis.

### Optional memory and synthesis assistance

If retrieval, source integration, or recurring synthesis takes substantial work, you can also use a memory tool. [Hindsight](../hindsight.md) is one implementation. It retains sources, integrates observations, and produces accounts from questions. Use the model to configure and check the tool and bring the adopted results back into the six accounts. Compare assistance by how it improves the work you actually do. The six questions, attributable grounds, consistency across the accounts, and correction through use apply in either case.
