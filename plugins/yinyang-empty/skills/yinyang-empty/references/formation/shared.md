# Shared Writing Principles for the Six Accounts

[简体中文](shared.zh-CN.md) · [Writing the six accounts](overview.md)

The six accounts are working descriptions for a person and a model. Write
each to answer its particular question about a shared practice. Use adopted
philosophy for concepts and reasons. Use situated material for the claims that
depend on this person, counterpart, relationship, or environment.

## Write from attributable evidence

Gather sources that can change the answer: an original statement, a correction,
work and its result, the conditions of a tool or interface, or a relevant
philosophical argument. Preserve enough of the speaker, occasion, conditions,
and original location for a reader to recover the grounds. Use a summary to
locate an original and judge a material interpretation in its context.

Distinguish what someone reported, what happened under inspected conditions,
what the partners adopt as a direction, and what they infer. A person finding
an interface tiring supports a report of effort. A completed task shows what
happened on that occasion. A claim about the cause of the effort or lasting
change in capability needs grounds of its own. Attribute conflicting accounts
and retain uncertainty where it can change a decision.

## Write for the practice the accounts support

State the activity, the relationship that matters, the conditions under which
it holds, and what follows in work. Give enough of the reason that another
reader can reconsider it. Keep extended arguments and unique evidence with
their sources. A literary image can reveal a relationship. Where that insight
is meant to guide action, describe what the reader can recognize and do with it.

Condense after understanding the material. Keep distinctions, counterexamples,
and dependencies that would change a judgment. For example, “Give concise
feedback” leaves the wanted contribution unspecified. A paragraph grounded in
an actual exercise could explain that the person offers a first interpretation
before feedback, the model gives one consequential counterexample drawn from
the passage, and the person chooses how to answer it. The arrangement makes
the purpose and next action intelligible and leaves room for alternatives.

Develop the person's adopted concepts and relations directly. Check each
addition for whose purpose it expresses and what changes if it becomes
standing guidance. A model's own operating conditions belong to the host or
tool that enforces them. A substantive objection belongs with its particular
claim and evidence. Preserve the person's choices and mark possibilities that
remain proposals.

## Read the six together

Compare accounts that describe the same object, participant, event, or outcome.
Check whether the same terms mean the same thing there, whether the conditions
are compatible, and whether an expected contribution has a route to become
observable. A sibling account is another current interpretation. Check material
facts against their independent grounds.

Keep each paragraph where it answers the reader's question. Link related
accounts and sources where they carry a different part of the reasoning.
For multiple language editions, check that the same claims, qualifications,
attributions, and usable locators survive. Keep personal evidence within the
audience entitled to receive it. If an important correction changes how the
accounts should be used, return to the [actual revision method](revision-method.md)
and follow the affected relationships through their real setting.
