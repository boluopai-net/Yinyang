# Teaching methods

Use this branch for skimming support and practice design, preparation or feedback.
The root Skill already supplies explanation, correction, evidence and State rules.

Use [Course materials](course-materials.md) when the encounter needs a
course, book or paper companion. Use [Learning-path governance](learning-path-governance.md#author-a-practice-path)
to organize a practice path. Here, design the encounter around its purpose,
source material and observable result.

## Select and support skimming

Apply this section when an original-reading pass is selected. Philosophical
text study normally enters through the learner's question and model-supported
source explanation under the philosophy branch; do not assign a preliminary
reading pass merely because a book is the source.

Choose the skimming span from the learner's intended onward reading and the connections needed to make it readable. One reference and pass may blend review of previously studied material with preview of unfamiliar or deeper material, across connected sources. Source-specific exposure adjusts selection and explanation; it does not partition the route into disjoint preview/review plans or exclude the new material that gives the review its purpose. Restore useful earlier connections without requiring mastery before continuing. Prior study remains evidence of exposure, not proof of current ease of reading. Arrange useful stages, such as an overall map followed by selected passages, without a fixed number of rounds. Anchor targeted explanations to original locations; a local detailed answer does not force the whole course into detailed-study mode.

Bind each pass to the actual source edition and selected scope, not a permanent course flag. Keep a suitable reference through ordinary reading, teaching, and practice. Revise it when sources or depth/scope change, the selection proves discontinuous or excessive, or the learner asks to reread. Identify the changed scope and new entry while preserving prior exposure and learning evidence. Different books/courses within one subject need their own exposure judgment; overlap can reduce the new selection. Return visits after later study may add a bounded reading pass without resetting the earlier one.

On entry or resumption, reconcile reported reading exposure with the conversation and Course State. Preserve explicit exemptions and evidenced prior passes. Inspect the guide, selected edition and actual passages across the selected scope. Keep the reading selection in the main guide, using an existing reading reference as a direct entry where it already serves that role. Choose a coherent, manageable traversal across the scope with necessary connections and representative figures or examples. Give verified ranges or headings, why each belongs, what the learner should notice, and where to resume after a skip. Mark unresolved source access or continuity gaps.

Use the original material as the reading surface, with brief orientation and
targeted answers. A learner can continue a skimming pass while leaving an
important concept provisional and saving its question for detailed study.
Course State records exposure, reading mode and deferred questions. The
reference records the selection and points to State. Preserve observed
detailed-study progress. Resume detailed study after the pass or at the
learner's choice, and accommodate a local question at its useful depth.

## Teach through practice

Follow the root's selected learning mode. On course entry or resumption, use
the [course training method](course-materials.md#mathematics-and-technical-study-three-connected-documents)
to establish readiness for the selected scope, including while teaching in
another mode. Preserve the recipient's course exceptions; question-led text
study follows the philosophy branch. Address learner-requested practice where
it serves the question.

Keep broad preparation available and work on a very small subset in the current
assignment. Select it by the purpose, consequential dependencies and observed
difficulties. Explain why the chosen points matter and connect them to the
whole. Give an important foundation enough varied practice to develop the
learner's judgment, then adjust when needs change.

Concentrate diagrams, contrasts, worked explanations and smaller source
exercises on the selected difficulties. Check question conditions, prerequisites
and the answer or evaluation grounds before assigning the item. Prepare
needed language support. Follow the [exercise and experiment guidance](course-materials.md#mathematics-and-technical-study-three-connected-documents) and
keep reusable reference answers with their source unit. Identify the source,
answer evidence and whether the teacher authored any support. Record actual
presentations and attempts under the shared State method.

After skimming, detailed study develops modeling through explanation and
learner attempts with feedback. Recover a familiar concept's intuition when it survives, and build unfamiliar
intuition through enough varied use. Judge familiarity per concept from current
responses, not from a course label or an earlier completion record. A compact
diagnostic check can locate a gap; it does not supply the practice needed to
close that gap.

Choose a worthwhile attempt that can reveal how the learner understands the
object, its relations, conditions, predictions or possible revisions. Develop a
partial intuition with candidates, structures, images or formalization and check
a learner's formal argument or correction when offered. Use recall or formal
reproduction costs to locate where support can help. Continue explanation as
needed and use the observed attempt to choose further practice.

Distinguish the material's learning role from its execution needs. For existing
exercises, select and read the actual problem, provide its wording and needed
context, invite a useful intuitive response, and give source-grounded feedback.
Supply formal work or candidate approaches when needed without requiring a full
answer before help. Preserve the original language and notation; provide
translation or explanation in the recipient's selected teaching language
when needed, without changing the problem's conditions or
mistaking language difficulty for conceptual difficulty. Use an answer form
that works in the current conversation; the teacher owns clear presentation,
not the learner a new exercise-design or tool-setup burden.

In engineering or project work, locate the selected attempt in the actual task
and provide the materials needed to make it. Depending on its learning value,
the learner might build a small example, interpret a model-generated result,
or judge a specific choice while the model carries other operations.

In feedback, identify the decisive relation, assumption or choice in the
response. Preserve what works and explain why a remaining step works or fails.
Choose the next explanation or practice from the gap. When it matters to judge
transfer, vary a relevant condition or context.

Vary the relevant representation, data, assumption, or context enough to
establish usable intuition and transfer; do not impose a fixed exercise count
or require every form for every concept. Keep necessary conditions and
counterexamples. Reduce repetition when responses show it adds no learning;
omit peripheral edge cases and mechanics without distinct conceptual value.
The model can recover facts, formulas, APIs, versions, and syntax, and carry
formal calculation and routine implementation without reducing conceptual depth
or the learning-bearing practice. Short turns manage
presentation load, not the total practice budget. Respect explicit course
exceptions such as free philosophy reading.

Classify the smallest operation, not Homework/Lab/notebook/Project labels.
Written practice needs no execution; execution of a learner practice slice is
local, never simulated. Teacher-owned source inspection, visual explanation,
and saved-document validation remain permitted teaching tools. Keep
selected intuitive contributions available for practice; the model also supplies
analysis, candidate explanations, synthesis, and checks. Model-led execution exposes choices,
outputs, failures, limits, or consequences. Mix allocations; skip redundant
mechanics.

A question, task or experiment supplies evidence; evaluation interprets it for
the learning purpose. Keep the problem conditions, reference answer or reasoned
alternatives, and evaluation grounds for reuse. Record what the learner judged,
material model support and untested scope. Assisted use or transfer can evidence
modeling when the learner makes the relevant judgment. A demonstration or a
passing run supplies evidence about the example or implementation. If recall
later changes, recover the materials and review or retest while preserving prior
evidence.

Use a formal checkpoint when the course mapping or assessment calls for
consolidating evidence across concepts. Choose the intuitive attempts that
reveal those relationships and use the root's support throughout inquiry.
Apply actual assignment or external assessment constraints when selecting
assistance. Let observed gaps guide the amount of ongoing practice.

For local practice, follow the guide's link to the applicable training unit and make the
slice, learning value, allocation, operation and observable evidence clear.
The active Local Teacher carries authorized execution under the project owner.
A Web teacher describing that slice retains the proposal and evidence boundary:
do not command another teacher or claim unobserved local completion. Neither
teacher creates a synchronization or handoff requirement for ordinary learning.
